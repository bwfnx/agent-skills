# Review Memo Structure

Build with the `docx` skill. Severity order, not document order.

## Header block

A small table: To / From / Date / Re / **Deadline**. Put the operative deadline in the header — including the day of week and any weekend or holiday shift — because it reframes everything that follows as time-bound.

## Opening (3–4 short paragraphs, no heading)

1. State that you verified against the live solicitation rather than working from memory, and why that mattered this time.
2. One honest sentence on what's genuinely good. Naming a strong section tells the applicant where *not* to spend remaining time.
3. The shape of the problem in one sentence — how many blockers, plus the one structural issue.
4. Any single fact that changes their planning assumptions (a lost late-submission window, an expired invitation, a cap they're near).

## Part 1 — Blockers

Two to four items maximum. Things that stop the submission or get it returned. Each gets:

- A heading that states the problem, not the topic. "Your biosketches are in a retired format and will fail the upload" — not "Biosketches."
- The quoted requirement, with the source
- What's wrong in the current draft, specifically
- The fix

## Part 2 — The one big judgment call

If the proposal turns on a single determination — clinical trial status, SBIR vs STTR, whether a pre-application invitation is valid, Direct-to-Phase-II feasibility evidence — give it its own part. Show the reasoning, name the risk on each side, and give one recommendation.

## Part 3 — Arithmetic that doesn't close

Timeline, enrollment, budget splits, period of performance. Show the math. Then fixes in priority order as a numbered list.

## Part 4 — How reviewers will score this

One subsection per evaluation criterion, using the agency's own criterion names. Lead each with the criterion's literal question in quotes, then answer it as a reviewer would.

Order these by how much damage each does, not by the order in the solicitation. Say plainly which one costs the most points.

## Part 5 — Domain-specific gaps

Human subjects, regulatory, security, data management — whatever applies. Short subsections, each naming a concrete omission and its fix.

## Part 6 — Compliance checklist

A two-column table: Item / What the solicitation requires. This is where the smaller binary items go — appendix rules, prohibited attachments, fee, page limits, entity name consistency, caps, registrations. Fifteen or so rows is normal.

## Part 7 — What I'd do, week by week

Three subsections with real date ranges: this week (blockers only), the middle stretch (content), the final stretch (submission mechanics). Bulleted, imperative, each item something that can be finished in a sitting.

Include a submit-early date distinct from the deadline, and say why.

## Closing

Three short paragraphs: what's genuinely strong and why it's worth funding; what's missing framed as additions rather than a rewrite; and the honest call on whether the timeline is achievable, naming the next cycle's deadline as a real option if it isn't.

Close with an open door.

## Sources

A bulleted list of every solicitation, notice, and policy document you actually used, with numbers and dates. State that everything was verified as of today's date and name anything you could not verify.

---

## Companion email

Also draft a short email the advisor can send. Not a summary of the memo — a triage note. Three blockers, the one judgment call, the single highest-value fix, the deadline, and one line pointing to the attached memo. Under 400 words. It should be sendable without editing.

Flag for the advisor anything in the memo that will land better on a call than in writing — particularly candid assessments of team members.
