# Verification Protocol

What to extract before critiquing. Read `agency-source-map.md` for where; this file is what.

## A. From the solicitation itself

**Deadlines**
- The operative due date, and whether it shifts for weekends or federal holidays (some agencies roll forward, some don't)
- Whether a late-submission window exists for this mechanism — several have eliminated it
- For rolling or multi-release programs: which release is open and when it closes
- Any pre-application deadline (pitch, letter of intent, invitation expiry) that has already passed

**Components**
- Every **required** attachment for this specific phase and mechanism
- Every **prohibited** attachment. Applicants never anticipate this category and it is as damaging as omission — submitting a commercialization plan where one is barred, or a regulatory attachment where one is forbidden, is a compliance failure.
- Page, character, and word limits per component
- Formatting rules that trigger return without review: font family and minimum size, margins, line spacing, column layout, pagination, file encryption
- Whether appendices or supplementary material are permitted at all

**Money and time**
- Award ceiling for this phase, including whether it's inclusive of fee, indirect costs, and technical assistance
- Sub-office or component-specific ceilings, which frequently exceed the government-wide guideline — applicants under-budget out of caution and leave money unspent
- Period of performance limits, including whether base and option periods must be exact durations
- Whether technical and business assistance funds may be requested and how
- Whether a fee is allowed and at what percentage

**Evaluation**
- The literal text of every evaluation criterion
- Their stated order of importance or weighting, if given
- Any criterion that applies only to this phase — phase-specific questions are often embedded inside general criteria and read like boilerplate until you notice they ask something concrete
- Whether cost is evaluated and how

**Scope**
- Whether the proposal must respond to a specific topic and subtopic
- Published scope exclusions — categories of work the agency will not fund regardless of quality
- Whether a technical point of contact should have been engaged, and whether that window has closed

## B. Government-wide rules (verify current values, don't assume)

These come from the SBA Policy Directive and statute and apply across agencies, but the numbers move and legislation amends them:

- Employee cap, counted with affiliates
- Ownership requirement — the percentage that must be held by US citizens or permanent residents
- Whether venture capital / hedge fund / private equity majority ownership is permitted, at which agencies, up to what share of that agency's funds, and whether it is barred under STTR
- PI primary employment requirement, and how it differs for STTR
- Work-percentage splits — SBIR Phase I and Phase II minimums performed by the small business; STTR's split between the company and the research institution
- Whether all R&D must be performed in the United States, and what the exception process is
- Current statutory guideline dollar amounts for Phase I and Phase II, and whether the agency may exceed them by waiver
- Data rights protection period
- **Performance benchmarks** — experienced firms above defined award-count thresholds must maintain minimum Phase I→II transition and commercialization rates or become ineligible to submit for a year. Check whether the applicant is anywhere near the trigger thresholds.

## C. Registrations

Confirm status rather than assuming. Lead times are long and these block submission:

- SAM.gov entity registration and UEI — takes days to weeks, and requires annual renewal
- SBIR.gov company registry and the SBC Control ID — separate from and additional to SAM
- The agency portal account (eRA Commons, DSIP with Login.gov, Research.gov, NSPIRES, AMP)
- Grants.gov, for grant agencies
- Any individual identifier required for key personnel — some agencies now require ORCID linked to the portal account, which is the one applicants miss

Also verify the legal entity name matches across every system. Mismatches surface late and painfully.

## D. Foreign risk and disclosure

Every agency now runs some form of due diligence, and requirements have tightened. Verify current scope, then check the proposal against it:

- Which disclosure form or attachment is required and where it goes
- Whether omitting it is fatal (at some agencies it is — the proposal is deemed noncompliant and receives no evaluation)
- What must be disclosed: ownership and control by foreign entities, joint ventures and subsidiaries, financial obligations to foreign state-owned enterprises, technology licensing or IP sales to countries of concern within a lookback period, investor composition, participation in malign foreign talent recruitment programs, research-institution affiliations
- Which watchlists the agency screens against
- Whether any team member, subsidiary, or investor triggers a disclosure the proposal doesn't make

Agencies increasingly use open-source analysis to detect **non-disclosure** rather than relying on self-report. An omission is a worse outcome than a disclosure requiring mitigation.

Separately, check whether foreign subawards or consortium agreements are permitted at all — some agencies now prohibit them outright while still allowing foreign consultants. The distinction is a budget-line question with a fatal downside if it's gotten wrong.

## E. Structural traps by proposal type

**STTR mis-filed as SBIR** — different work splits, requires a research institution partner and an intellectual property agreement, and the PI employment rule differs.

**Direct to Phase II / Fast-Track** — requires documented Phase I-equivalent feasibility achieved with non-program funding. If that documentation is thin, the proposal is typically not evaluated at all rather than scored low.

**Resubmission** — check whether the agency permits it, whether a response-to-review document is required or forbidden, and whether the applicant has used it correctly.

**Per-company submission caps** — agencies are moving toward limits on proposals per company per fiscal year, per solicitation, or per topic. Verify whether a cap applies and whether the applicant is at risk of exceeding it across their whole portfolio, not just this proposal.

## F. Recording gaps

Track what you could not verify and surface it. Typical: a solicitation reachable only via mirror, a limit stated inconsistently across pages, a portal behind login whose rules you could not read, a requirement that appears to have changed without a locatable notice.

Name those specifically and tell the applicant to confirm them with the program officer or technical point of contact before submitting.
